# Differentiators and strict submission audit

Judgement as of **17 October 2026**. Effort/impact labels are qualitative estimates for this solo project. Novelty and adoption remain hypotheses.

## Feature priorities

| Rank | Candidate differentiator | Effort | Potential impact | Decision |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dated source trail, raw IDs and explicit unknowns | Low–medium | High for an evidence-review task | **Submission feature 1.** Implemented, including a real SRA desk case; validate against existing exports. |
| 2 | Tentative match/conflict review with no automatic merge | Medium | High where a walkthrough establishes reconciliation work | **Submission feature 2.** Implemented evidence handling. No legal equivalence or truth adjudication. |

| 6 | Community co-design and assisted challenge of records | Medium | Potentially high | Prepare consented workflow; no co-design claim until participation occurs. |
| 7 | Repeat service observations linked to existing complaint IDs | Medium–high | Potentially high with a responsible operator | Trial extension only; do not create another grievance inbox. |
| 8 | Maintenance-cost and coverage comparison | Medium–high for real inputs | Medium | Hypothetical tool only until quotations, feasibility and coverage are validated. |
| 9 | Satellite morphology/change context | High | Low for this review task | Cut training and service-access inference. Broad imagery does not establish household conditions. |
| 10 | Digital household registry, allocation ledger or relocation/rental decision engine | Very high | Unproven here, substantial governance dependency | Cut from this submission. Tenure/rental needs professional and resident review, not automated decisions. |

The three selected features reflect **actual data readiness**. If a valid service survey and network become available, priorities/access can replace document search in the pitch. Do not list a feature merely because it sounds advanced.

## Before and proposed after

Before: a reviewer uses the existing portal, GIS or spreadsheet and seeks missing supporting records. Actual effort and defects are unmeasured. Proposed after: the same review receives a reproducible brief with dated sources, unresolved matches and explicit unknowns. Whether this changes quality or time must be tested. The three-row case demonstrates correct caution, not unmet demand.

## Five fixes in priority order

1. **Validate demand:** observe one existing ward/operator review and compare the actual export. Reject the gap if it is already solved adequately.
2. **Acquire current, lawful local evidence:** authoritative IDs, operator responsibility and repeated asset observations. The real listing audit cannot stand in for service measurements.
3. **Finish browser acceptance:** run the retained checks in an environment with a browser. No live-demo reliability claim before this.
4. **Check submission compliance privately:** official template, actual field/upload limits, identity, domicile/category and current deadline. Personal data were intentionally omitted.
5. **Replace financial assumptions and assign O&M:** obtain quotations and a competent recurring payer before a work order or expansion.

If time is short, retain the real record case, source/date/unknown workflow, evidence search and a clearly labelled analytical fixture. Cut extra maps, custom AI, a new complaint portal, household registry and broad redevelopment finance. Rehearse the honest evidence boundary instead of adding features.
