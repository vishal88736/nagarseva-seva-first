## 1. Project title

**6 words / 15-word draft budget.**

Seva Evidence: Source-linked service inspection review

## 2. Sector and sub-theme

**9 words / 25-word draft budget.**

Urban Development. Affordable housing, slum redevelopment and basic-service assurance.

## 3. One-line value proposition

**25 words / 40-word draft budget.**

Help a ward reviewer assemble dated service evidence, identify uncertain record matches and explain the next inspection without converting missing data into claims about residents.

## 4. Problem statement

**98 words / 150-word draft budget.**

Housing delivery and mapped infrastructure do not establish reliable neighbourhood services. Mumbai research documents water reliability, maintenance and post-relocation governance concerns. For a proposed M/E cluster, the working question is whether reviewers can assemble geographically matched, dated evidence for the next service inspection. A real three-row SRA desk case illustrates linkage caution: two records share CTS 1-A, while a third uses 1 A. The inspected listing provides proposal placeholders and no status or update-date columns. These facts justify checking evidence, but do not establish delays, service failures or inadequate internal government systems. Current local service prevalence remains unmeasured.

## 6. Proposed solution

**107 words / 150-word draft budget.**

Seva Evidence is a local inspection-review workbench. It preserves sources, dates, raw identifiers and unknown values. A real public-record audit keeps three SRA schemes distinct and flags tentative CTS associations for authoritative review. Separate analytical tools calculate graph routes, show service-deficit score intervals and weight sensitivity, preserve conflicting claims, compare hypothetical maintenance options and search curated research cards with citations and abstention. Imports stay in browser memory; exports retain provenance and assumptions. Real references and synthetic tests remain separate. The next product test is one existing officer/operator review, comparing evidence quality and effort. The software does not allocate housing, decide eligibility, recommend relocation or certify service functionality.

## 7. Innovation and existing alternatives

**95 words / 150-word draft budget.**

The proposed contribution is an auditable local review workflow that makes uncertain matches, conflicting reports and missing evidence explicit. It is a hypothesis about incremental usefulness, not a claim that GIS or complaint tracking is new. SRA already has GIS, PMAY specifies MIS and infrastructure monitoring, SBM-U 2.0 specifies functional-asset monitoring, and Swachhata supports confirmation and reopening. Community GIS and scorecards also have established prior art. A spreadsheet can reproduce the three-row record audit. The prototype must therefore demonstrate better review quality or effort against the actual existing process before claiming differentiation or adoption value.

Supporting sources, accessed 17 October 2026: [source 1](https://maharashtra.nic.in/news/gis-sra/), [source 2](https://mahadma.maharashtra.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/swachh-bharat-2.pdf), [source 3](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2162194&lang=2&reg=48).

## 8. Technology and methodology

**101 words / 150-word draft budget.**

The prototype uses dependency-free browser JavaScript, GeoJSON-shaped aggregate inputs, a walking graph with Dijkstra shortest paths, explicit weighted scoring with missing-value bounds, sensitivity checks, and bounded exact enumeration for hypothetical maintenance coverage. BM25-style lexical retrieval searches 14 curated source cards; it is not a trained AI model or full-document question-answering system. Schema checks reject invalid dates, unsafe source URLs and recognised sensitive fields. Source hashes support reproducibility. Original inputs and tentative crosswalks remain inspectable. No computer vision, satellite poverty inference, automatic eligibility decision or relocation optimisation is used. Production identity, shared-case storage, approved integration and complete privacy controls remain future dependencies.

## 10. Implementation and feasibility

**103 words / 150-word draft budget.**

A proposed 90-day trial begins with a sponsor, lawful current asset records, an agreed cluster boundary and observation protocol. By day 30, document the existing review process and register up to 20 public assets. By day 60, aim for three rounds totalling 60 dated observations, preserving failed visits and disagreements. By day 90, compare briefs with the current process using independent review, task time and critical evidence omissions. These are proposed targets. Proceed only with an accountable operator and safe data handling. Start with approved file exports; promise no live government API integration. Broader deployment requires browser, accessibility, security, data-sharing and maintenance validation.

## 12. Expected impact and measurement

**91 words / 100-word draft budget.**

Expected benefits are hypotheses: fewer critical evidence omissions, clearer handling of unknowns/conflicts and lower effort for a defined review. Measure paired task time, source traceability, independently checked observations, unresolved disagreements and repeat failures in the agreed trial. No improvement percentage or service-reliability baseline is claimed. Separate software correctness from field accuracy and resident outcomes. Include women, older and disabled residents, renters and migrants through voluntary accessible participation. The proposal aligns with SDG 6 service reliability, SDG 11 inclusive urban services and SDG 1 attention to deprivation, without claiming measured SDG progress.

## 15. Inclusion and safeguards

**89 words / 100-word draft budget.**

Use public asset or aggregate evidence and voluntary resident feedback. Do not collect Aadhaar, household eligibility labels, names, phone numbers, income or health records in the public demo. Provide an assisted route for people without smartphones and review opening hours, fees, safety and disability barriers separately from distance. Keep conflicting reports and unknown dates visible. Imported information stays in tab memory, but current field-name checks are not complete privacy protection. Production deployment needs approved access, retention and publication rules. The tool makes no automated housing, allocation or relocation decisions.

## 16. Risks and mitigation

**93 words / 100-word draft budget.**

The main risks are duplication of existing systems, inaccessible or old inputs, false CTS matches, unverified service claims and insufficient solo maintenance capacity. Validate the existing workflow first, obtain the smallest lawful current extract, retain original identifiers and dates, and require authoritative review of tentative matches. Withhold deprivation ranks when observations are absent. Keep operator claims distinct from independent observations. Begin with one bounded task and approved exports. Stop expansion without an operating owner, recurring budget and evidence of incremental usefulness. Browser and field validation remain necessary before claiming end-to-end or operational readiness.
