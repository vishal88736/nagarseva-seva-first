# Problem evidence, candidate gaps and location decision

Access date: **17 October 2026**. This document distinguishes verified observations, interpretations and untested product hypotheses. Confidence refers to the stated proposition, not to how useful a proposed application will be.

## The problem that is supported

**Housing provision and reliable, accessible neighbourhood services are related but separate delivery tasks.** Historical official data and Mumbai research establish service and livelihood problems; current policy recognises stalled redevelopment and infrastructure constraints. They do not establish the present failure rate in any selected settlement. [Maharashtra Housing Policy 2025, §3.15.14](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s30bf727e907c5fc9d5356f11e4c45d613/uploads/2025/02/202507311134926775.pdf), [Chaskin et al. (2024)](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/records/c0z96-tzt75)

The sharp **working problem** is: *For a small settlement cluster in Mumbai M/E, can the ward team and community operator assemble a dated, checkable account of essential-service conditions and access, so they can choose the next inspection or service improvement?* The difficulty of doing this in the current workflow is a hypothesis to test. Do not claim a demonstrated cross-departmental failure before that test.

## Five proposed research gaps: challenge each one

### G1 — Fragmented housing/service evidence

- **Proposition:** a local reviewer may struggle to combine scheme records, assets and service observations with compatible dates and identifiers.
- **Support:** separate public SRA, MHADA, municipal and mission interfaces exist; visible SRA rows do not themselves contain operational water/toilet observations. This establishes multiple interfaces, not failed internal integration. [SRA public records](https://dms.sra.gov.in/SRAPUBLIC/), [MHADA GIS](https://gis.mhada.gov.in/)
- **Counterevidence / alternatives:** NIC already documents a multi-layer SRA GIS; PMAY specifies a unified MIS; municipal GIS and NGO systems may already exchange the relevant information. [NIC SRA](https://maharashtra.nic.in/news/gis-sra/)
- **Assessment:** **fact**: multiple public interfaces. **Inference**: evidence reconciliation could help. **Hypothesis**: existing staff lack it. Confidence **Moderate** in an access/reconciliation problem for this desk research; **Low** in an unmet officer need.
- **Validation:** observe one review end to end; request a de-identified report and data dictionary; count actual unmatched IDs, contradictory dates and time spent. Reject the gap if the existing export already supplies a usable answer.

### G2 — Transparent prioritisation of service inspections

- **Proposition:** an auditable priority list may help allocate scarce inspection/service-improvement effort.
- **Support:** published Mumbai health triage demonstrates that observable settlement conditions can inform prioritisation, while showing limitations. It does not validate new criteria. [Osrin et al. (2011)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11524-011-9556-7)
- **Counterevidence / alternatives:** scorecards, community GIS, statutory priorities, official field judgement and simple first-in-first-out queues already exist. A weighted score is established MCDA, not novel AI. [Malczewski (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1080/13658810600661508)
- **Assessment:** **fact**: prior art exists. **Hypothesis**: exposing uncertainty and ranking instability would improve this local decision. Confidence **Low–Moderate** pending workflow and independent labels.
- **Validation:** agree one decision and its constraints with residents/officers; compare with their current process on the same cases; test sensitivity and false negatives. If small weight changes reverse priorities or missingness dominates, show an inspection-needed list instead of a definitive ranking.

### G3 — Comparing improvement, redevelopment and relocation

- **Proposition:** a local appraisal may benefit from showing access, cost and livelihood consequences side by side.
- **Support:** displacement/livelihood and maintenance research establishes relevant consequences; housing policy acknowledges financial delivery constraints. [Ramanath (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.worlddev.2016.04.007), [2025 housing policy](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s30bf727e907c5fc9d5356f11e4c45d613/uploads/2025/02/202507311134926775.pdf)
- **Counterevidence / alternatives:** planning studies, development plans, DCPR appraisals and participatory enumerations already compare land/use constraints. Location distance alone does not capture compensation, social networks, tenure or household preferences.
- **Assessment:** **inference**: a transparent comparison could aid discussion. Confidence **Moderate** in relevance, **Low** in data readiness or proof that current professional appraisal is inadequate.
- **Validation:** obtain actual site options, cost estimates, land/planning constraints and resident preferences. Until then, label all scenarios hypothetical. No automated eligibility, allocation or relocation recommendation.

### G4 — Completion versus ongoing service reliability

- **Proposition:** periodic, independently checkable observations may reveal persistent operational problems after construction/handover.
- **Support:** water and relocation studies distinguish assets from actual service conditions; BMC documents CBO sanitation maintenance arrangements. [BMC Year Book](https://portal.mcgm.gov.in/irj/go/km/docs/documents/HomePage%20Data/Whats%20New/English%20Year%20Book%202025%20Portal.pdf)
- **Counterevidence / alternatives:** PMAY includes infrastructure; AMRUT includes outcomes/quality mechanisms. Follow-up verified that SBM-U 2.0 explicitly specifies functional-asset monitoring and an integrated ward/asset platform. Existing inspections may already answer the need. [PMAY](https://pmaymis.gov.in/PMAYMIS2_2024/Operational-Guidelines-of-PMAY-U-2.pdf), [AMRUT](https://amrut.mohua.gov.in/uploads/AMRUT_2.0_Operational_Guidelines.pdf), [SBM Chapter 10](https://mahadma.maharashtra.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/swachh-bharat-2.pdf)
- **Assessment:** **fact**: asset completion alone is not a measurement of continuous service. **Hypothesis**: a local evidence trail is missing or unreliable. Confidence **High** in the distinction; **Low–Moderate** in the incremental product gap.
- **Validation:** compare existing O&M logs, contractor claims and independent repeat observations for the same asset and time window. Identify the authority/operator able to act. Stop if no lawful records or accountable owner can be obtained.

### G5 — Citizen verification of complaint resolution

- **Proposition:** residents may need clearer follow-up or independent verification of persistent/reopened issues.
- **Support:** responsible post-handover governance matters; current local closure accuracy is unknown.
- **Counterevidence / alternatives:** **Swachhata already supports citizen confirmation and reopening.** BMC and state grievance channels exist. [PIB on Swachhata](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2162194&lang=2&reg=48)
- **Assessment:** confidence **High** that a generic “geo-photo complaint + reopen” novelty claim is indefensible. Confidence **Low** that a new local verification product is needed.
- **Validation:** with permission, examine a de-identified set of closed/reopened cases and current routing. Test whether existing IDs plus repeat observations add useful evidence. Do not create another complaint inbox unless the authority requests and owns it.

### Most defensible gap to test

**A small, reproducible process for assembling dated, geographically matched evidence and showing uncertainty before a service inspection/review.** Its potential contribution is the quality and usefulness of that process in a specific local workflow. It is **not an established worldwide feature gap**. Falsify it early: if current systems already provide the same evidence with acceptable effort, improve an export/checklist or change the project.

## People and decisions to validate

These are **provisional research personas**, not people interviewed:

| Stakeholder | Decision/need to investigate | Evidence to request without unnecessary personal data |
|---|---|---|
| Woman resident, older/disabled resident, renter or migrant | Can I safely and affordably use the service, and who follows up? | Voluntary accounts of hours, queues, fees, route barriers and unresolved issues; avoid citizenship/eligibility inference |
| BMC ward/service officer | What warrants inspection, which team owns the work and what proof closes it? | De-identified inspection/complaint workflow and service standards |
| SRA/MHADA officer | Which applicable project/site record is authoritative? | Scheme IDs, dated approvals/status and permitted spatial references |
| CBO/operator/contractor | What must I maintain, report or demonstrate? | Asset register, actual MoU/contract, maintenance and repair evidence |
| NGO/community group | Are missing people and disputed conditions represented safely? | Survey definitions, consent process, aggregate validations and data-sharing conditions |
| Financier/funder | Is the proposed intervention affordable over its life? | Actual capex/O&M commitments, cost estimates and responsibility; no invented bankability or CSR commitment |

## One-week decision gates and cuts

1. **Within the first build day:** secure a small lawful spatial input, date/metadata and one demonstrable decision. Otherwise switch to a clearly labelled document/evidence audit rather than presenting fabricated service-gap maps.
2. Demonstrate source traceability, one amenity-access calculation and missing-evidence handling. Add prioritisation only if comparable service observations exist.
3. Seek one workflow review if feasible; without it, submit a desk prototype with an explicit validation plan.

Cut drone surveys, custom satellite segmentation, blockchain allocation, automated relocation, city-wide household registration, a new grievance app and a comprehensive financial engine. These add dependency or duplication without establishing local value. See [technical_feasibility.md](technical_feasibility.md) for the smaller build sequence.
